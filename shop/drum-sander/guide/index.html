Chapter 01

Stock

ST-01

Buy, acclimate, and measure the plywood

Know your real ply thickness before a single dado is cut. · step 1 of 14

Do this

  1. Stand the sheets on edge in the shop for at least 48 hours. Euro BB arrives at 18 mm, not ¾″.
  2. Caliper the ply in six places. Record the number as ply_actual (model default 0.75″).
  3. If it is not 0.75″, edit ply_actual in cad/walter_ds16.py and regenerate every sheet. Do not shave the 16.5″ inner span to make the old numbers work.
Before you move on · QC-01Measured ply thickness recorded. Inner span stays 16.5″ (419 mm).

Hold point. Nothing is cut yet. This is the cheapest place to catch the 18 mm surprise.

ST-02

Rough-cut the panels oversize

Flat, labelled, manageable blanks out of full sheets. · step 2 of 14

Do this

  1. Cut every panel about ⅛″ over width and ¼″ over length. Final size comes off one stop later.
  2. Write the part ID on each blank in pencil the moment it leaves the sheet.
  3. Keep the two side blanks as a matched pair — they get drilled together in ST-04.

⚠ Full sheets are heavy and awkward. Support the offcut so it cannot drop onto the blade or onto you.

Chapter 03

Sides

ST-03

Cut both sides to final size on one stop

Two panels that ARE the same size, not two panels that measure the same. · step 3 of 14

Do this

  1. Set the stop for 30″ and cut both panels (S-001). Do not move the stop between cuts.
  2. Reset for 22″ depth and cut both (S-002).
  3. Mark the INNER face and the INFEED edge on each panel. Those two marks are Datum C and Datum B for the rest of the build.
Before you move on · QC-02Panels identical within a pencil line; inner face and infeed edge marked on both.

ST-04

Stack-drill the pair: bearing, flange, pilots

One hole pattern, drilled once, so the drum axis cannot be crooked. · step 4 of 14

Do this

  1. Clamp the panels face-to-face, inner faces together, infeed edges flush.
  2. Lay out the bearing centreline at Y 11″ from the infeed edge and Z 18.5″ up from the bottom.
  3. Set the actual flange on the panel and transfer its bolt holes. The drawing shows 2.05″ square as a placeholder — your bearing decides.
  4. Drill the ⌀1.125″ shaft clearance and the four bolt holes through both panels at once (S-008).
  5. Drive side only: pilot the motor pivot at Y 4″ / Z 6″ and the indicator pad at Y 8″ / Z 16″.
Before you move on · QC-02Panels separated; hole patterns line up when the panels are flipped face-to-face.

Hold point. Stop here until the flange is in your hand. Do not drill this pattern from the drawing alone.

⚠ Clamp hard. A panel that shifts mid-drill gives you two different machines.

ST-05

Dado the stretcher housings and way rebates

Inner-face joinery, mirrored — the one operation where the panels are NOT identical. · step 5 of 14

Do this

  1. Split the pair. From here the panels are mirror images — work only on the marked inner faces.
  2. Cut three stretcher housings 0.25″ deep × 0.75″ wide × 4″ tall at stations IN-LO, OUT-LO, OUT-HI (J-001, S-006). Rails stand on edge. None of these dados may sit at Z ≈ 12 — that is the table.
  3. Rout the vertical way rebate 0.520″ deep × 2.5″ wide, Y 9.75–12.25″ (drum CL), Z 11–16.438″ (J-002, S-007).
  4. Test the dado width on an offcut of the same ply first. A sloppy housing is a racking frame.
Before you move on · QC-12 · QC-13Rebate 0.520″ deep ±0.010″; a scrap of way stock sits 0.230″ proud. Dados at IN-LO / OUT-LO / OUT-HI only.
⚠ Do not dado the panels while they are still stacked. You will get two left-hand sides.

Chapter 04

Frame

ST-06

Glue the box: stretchers, base, diagonals

A square, stiff carcase that will not rack when a board is pushed through it. · step 6 of 14

Do this

  1. Dry-fit all three stretchers (17″, housed 0.25″ each end) into IN-LO, OUT-LO, OUT-HI. Check the inner span reads 16.5″. Confirm no rail sits in the table's Z range (11.5–15.94″).
  2. Glue and clamp. Measure both diagonals and pull them equal before the glue grabs.
  3. Drill and drive #8 × 2″ screws from outside into each stretcher end.
  4. Screw the base deck on, then measure the diagonals again.
Before you move on · QC-03Diagonals equal within 1/32″. Inner span 16.5″ at top, middle, and bottom.

Hold point. Let the glue cure before hanging anything heavy on the box.

ST-07

Bond the vertical UHMW ways and wax them

Two plumb rails for the table shoes to wrap. These, not the stretchers, locate the table in X and Y. · step 7 of 14

Do this

  1. Cut two UHMW strips 5.438″ × 2.5″ and set them into the vertical rebates. They should project 0.230″.
  2. Bond and clamp. Optional: #8 flush screws from the outer face.
  3. Square both ways to the base. An indicator riding a tall square should read the same on left and right.
  4. Paste wax only. Never oil — oil migrates into the wood and into your finish.
Before you move on · QC-04Ways plumb. Projection 0.230″ ±0.010″. Centered on drum CL at Y 11″.

Chapter 05

Drum

ST-08

Pack-bore the discs and laminate the drum

One stiff cylinder blank on one true axis. · step 8 of 14

Do this

  1. Bandsaw 19 MDF discs and 2 birch ends at ⌀5.125″ — oversize on purpose.
  2. Stack the whole pack in the P-015 jig and ream ⌀0.75″ straight through (J-005, S-009). Never bore discs one at a time.
  3. Glue the stack with a 1 mm relief every 4 MDF discs. Birch ends outboard.
  4. Weigh the two end discs against each other and balance them before assembly.
Before you move onBore accepts the shaft with light friction. Stack length 15.75″.

Hold point. Full cure before the drum ever spins. A delaminated disc at 1035 RPM is a projectile.

⚠ MDF dust is the worst dust in the shop. Respirator and extraction on.

ST-09

Hang the shaft: drive FIXED, idler FLOATING

One bearing defines the axis; the other lets the shaft grow. · step 9 of 14

Do this

  1. Slide the 22.5″ shaft through the drum and both panels.
  2. Bolt H-001 to the drive side and torque it. That flange is now the drum-axis datum (J-006).
  3. Set H-002 on the 0.25″ pad on the idler side. Snug only — the shaft must still be able to slide axially (J-007).
  4. Spin the drum by hand through several turns. It should coast, not bind and not ring.
Before you move on · QC-11Shaft turns freely; measurable axial float at the idler end.
⚠ Locking both flanges bends the shaft and kills both bearings. Do not do it because it feels tighter.

Chapter 06

Drive

ST-10

Mount the motor, align the pulleys, lock the cradle

Belt tension by gravity, then locked so it cannot pump. · step 10 of 14

Do this

  1. Pivot P-012 on the drive-side hole and hang the 0.5 HP motor on it.
  2. Fit the 3″ motor pulley and the 5″ drum pulley. Lay a straightedge across both faces and shim until they are coplanar.
  3. Let the cradle hang to tension the belt, measure the centre distance, then buy the belt to that number.
  4. Lock the cradle. A cradle that still swings will pump the belt and chirp.
Before you move on · QC-09Pulley faces coplanar; belt tracks centred when the drum is turned by hand.

Hold point. Do not connect power yet.

⚠ Mains wiring, switch, and grounding belong to a qualified electrician and your local code. This package does not release electrical work.

ST-11

Build the torsion-box table and bond the wear face

A flat plate that stays flat. This is the surface you measure against forever. · step 11 of 14

Do this

  1. Glue the rib grid at 4″ o.c. between the two skins. Full glue, clamped on a flat reference.
  2. Check the box flat in both directions and on both diagonals. Flatten it before going further.
  3. Bond the phenolic or tooling-plate wear face on top (J-004).
  4. Groove and bolt P-017 shoes under each long edge, centered on Y 11″. Groove 0.240″ × 2.516″ wraps the way tongue (J-012).
  5. Confirm the finished plan size is 16″ × 22″ so the wear face still carries 15.5″ of work.

ST-12

Fit the dual Acme lift on the drum centerline

Both nuts sit under the cut. The table rises in Z without racking in X or Y. · step 12 of 14

Do this

  1. Screw both P-018 thrust blocks to the base at Y 11″ (drum CL), X left and right. Fit thrust washers and e-clips (J-013).
  2. Bolt a bronze nut block under the table, each nut on the same Y as its screw — not at the infeed and outfeed ends.
  3. Fit both ½-10 Acme screws. One turn is 0.1000″. Thirty turns is 3″ (CALC-003).
  4. Chain-couple the two screws. Fit the left clutch and P-019 home dog (J-014).
  5. Run the table through the full 3.25″ of travel. Shoes must stay wrapped; the table must not yaw.
Before you move on · QC-12 · QC-14Table rises and falls freely through full travel; both ends move the same amount; shoes stay captured.

Chapter 08

Hood & Hold-Downs

ST-13

Kerf-bend the hood and set the roller yokes

The guard that is also the dust hood, plus the rollers that kill snipe. · step 13 of 14

Do this

  1. Kerf-bend the hood blank around the drum arc, glue the form, fill the kerfs, and fit the 4″ port.
  2. Hang both roller yokes on shoulder-bolt pivots with light compression springs.
  3. Set each roller 0.030″ below the drum OD with paper on, using feeler gauges.
  4. Check the hood clears the drum, the rollers, and the oscillator stroke if you fitted one.

⚠ Hood ON is the primary guard. Open it only with the machine stopped and unplugged.

Chapter 09

Commissioning

ST-14

True, wrap, re-clock, and cut a witness board

Turn an assembled machine into a calibrated one. · step 14 of 14

Do this

  1. Paper off: true the drum with the full-width sled until TIR ≤ 0.002″ mid-span (S-010).
  2. Wrap Velcro, then spiral the paper. Paper thickness is not uniform, so parallel changes here.
  3. Paper on: indicate the drum at the drive end (A) and the idler end (B). Bring |A−B| ≤ 0.003″, then set the home dog (S-011).
  4. Sand a witness board at 80 grit, one pass. Caliper four corners. Scatter must be ≤ 0.003″.
  5. Then work the pass schedule: 0.008″ rough, 0.004″ medium, 0.001″ finish.
Before you move on · QC-06 · QC-07 · QC-10TIR ≤ 0.002″ paper-off · |A−B| ≤ 0.003″ paper-on · witness scatter ≤ 0.003″.

Hold point. First powered run: hood on, no stock, stand clear of the drum ends, hand on the switch.

⚠ Do not sand stock shorter than about 12″ without the sled. Hands never under the drum or the hold-downs.

Chapter 10

Commissioning and acceptance

Q-101

Inspection plan, calibration, and the numbers you record

Print this one and sign it. An uncalibrated drum sander makes tapered boards very efficiently.

Calibration sequence

  1. Disconnect power. Unplug. Hood off. Paper off for TIR; paper on for A/B parallel.
  2. Seat the table on the vertical ways. Raise/lower through full travel. Shoes stay wrapped. No bind, no yaw. Square on the wear face — no twist.
  3. Drum TIR. Indicator on mid-span OD. Rotate by hand. If > 0.002″, re-true on P-013 before wrapping.
  4. Wrap & re-clock. Velcro then spiral paper. Paper is not uniform — A/B will change. This is the measurement that matters.
  5. A/B parallel. Same indicator height, drive then idler. Uncouple left Acme; 1/10 turn ≈ 0.1000″. Recouple. Set home dog.
  6. Hold-down height. Feelers under each roller vs drum. 0.030″ below. Leading snipe → ease outfeed spring; trailing → ease infeed.
  7. Witness board. 6″ × 16″ maple, 80 grit, one pass. Ridge at overlap = idler high/low. Caliper corners. Log in the Build app.
  8. Taper mode (optional). Uncouple left, drop idler a few thousandths, sand, then return to home dog — do not re-invent parallel each time.
