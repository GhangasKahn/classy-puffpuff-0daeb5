# Joinery Illustration & Tolerance Standards

## Minimum Content for Every Critical Joint Sheet
1. Geometry block — exact sizes, housing depths, tenon dimensions, peg diameter and location, drawbore offset if used. Reference the chosen datum system (face/edge or centerline).
2. Assembly direction arrow and reference faces/datums or centerlines.
3. Numbered cut-and-fit sequence (optimized for hand tools when the package specifies hand-tool execution).
4. Acceptance / Stop criteria (numerical where possible).
5. Water control, end-grain sealing, and wood-movement notes.
6. Preferred view combination: primary orthographic + detail section or isometric for clarity.
7. For Japanese-influenced joints: traditional name (when useful) + precise modern geometry.

## Preferred Structural Joinery Hierarchy
1. Housed drawbored mortise-and-tenon (visible peg preferred for traditional and Japanese character).
2. Through-tenon with wedge or keyed retention.
3. Complex interlocking Japanese joints when higher resistance or specific aesthetic is required.
4. Mechanical fasteners with documented capacity (through-bolts with large washers/backing plates).
5. Glue only as secondary or for non-structural elements.

Never rely on glue alone for primary structural connections in exterior or high-load work.

## Standard Tolerance Language (use or adapt)
- Shoulder bearing: continuous contact, local gap ≤ 1/32"
- Post plumb: ≤ 1/8" over 60"
- Gate or frame diagonals: difference ≤ 1/8" before final fastening
- Peg fit: seats by hand or light mallet; no heavy striking
- Clearances: state hinge gap, latch gap, bottom clearance explicitly
- Centerline spacing (when used): ±1/8" post-to-post

## Drawbore Protocol (when used)
- Peg center typically 1 to 1-1/4" from shoulder
- Offset 1/16" toward the shoulder (standard starting point; adjust only with justification)
- Always dry-fit, transfer, and test with sacrificial peg before final riven peg
- Trim proud, chamfer, and seal end grain after driving; never leave a water-catching recess

## 3D-Print Prototyping Guidance
For any joint that is irreversible, complex, or high-value wood:
- Print critical geometry at 1:1 in PLA or PETG
- Verify dry fit, shoulder contact, and peg path before final wood
- Especially valuable for complex Japanese interlocking joints
- Note on the joint sheet: “3D prototype recommended before final wood”

## Material Rules (baseline)
- Reject twist, pith, decay, end checks that enter joinery, short grain at high-stress zones
- White oak preferred for structural outdoor members; cedar for non-structural infill
- Acclimate, rough oversize, rest, then establish one face A and edge B
- Prefinish inaccessible surfaces; keep pure bearing faces clean of finish buildup
- Never use ordinary ferrous fasteners or steel wool on oak/cedar (staining and corrosion)
