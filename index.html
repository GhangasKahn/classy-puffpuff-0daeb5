01

You already own the wrong knife.

The pack weight is honest. The work is not. A 140-gram drop-point will open an elk and then fail a dovetail. A marking knife will scribe a shoulder and then fail a trout. A fillet knife will skate a salmon and then fail a square. Each tool is telling the truth about one job and lying about the rest.

Hyperlite travel makes that lie expensive. Every extra ounce is a decision. So the market offers “ultralight hunters” that are still thick behind the edge, and “EDC sushi knives” that cannot register, and woodworking travel knives that were never asked to rinse blood and Niagara water out of a skeleton. The contradiction is not lack of enthusiasm. It is one bevel being asked to be three tools.

The river

Panfish skin, catfish slime, pinbones you want to feel, ikejime you will not do with the apex. Niagara Chinook that will not fit a 72 mm blade or a 0.8 mm wire.

The bench

A back that sits on a square. A bevel you can repeat. Kumiko, chamfers, layout lines. Flat. Not hollow. Not “close enough.”

The trail

Feather sticks, notches, food, controlled chopping into the work. Still a knife. Still not an axe, a pry bar, or a pelvis splitter.

02

Stop asking one edge to be universal.

SHINOBI//82 KAGE is a chassis with a wedge lock — a kusabi — and three blades that are allowed to disagree with each other.

Zen-Wu already laminates wear steel into titanium, already ships travel knives with flat backs and flat bevels for kumiko and trail work, already makes a PalmBlade that is a marking knife and a chisel in one palm. This proposal does not invent that language. It asks that language to go to the river without picking up plastic, and to come back to the bench without picking up a second handle.

The name is character, not costume: quiet, exact, unadvertised. No kanji as decoration. No tsuba. No locked index ring. Influence from Japanese single-bevel discipline and from JDM fishing hardware that treats a reel frame as a structural object — not from cinema assassins.

A perfectly flat ura and a 15° single flat bevel. No urasuki hollow. This is the geometry Zen-Wu already uses on travel knives and plane irons.

Mechanism

Kusabi-Lock

A titanium wedge seats the blade module in an open frame. No hidden screws in the food path. No adhesive. Rinse-through apertures follow load paths, not decoration: a dorsal compression rail, a ventral tension rail, radiused bridges against torsion. Kurogaki contact inlays clip on when the job is wet and long; they come off when the job is pinbones and layout.

The saya is the second handle. Bare metal is the precision mode. Snap the wood onto the skeleton and the palm gets an oval swell for catfish, deer, and elk seam work. Handle diameter changes muscular effort; a permanently razor-thin skeleton is not an honest all-day grip. The sheath was going to be carried anyway.

05

What may touch the work

  • Ti-6Al-4VFrame, wedge, collet, sharpening substrate. Open. Rinseable.
  • MagnaMaxMIZU candidate only. Public CATRA and saltwater work is Larrin Thomas’s, not ours. Production heat and Zen-Wu availability are unconfirmed.
  • MagnaCutNATA candidate. Zen-Wu already laminates CPM MagnaCut into titanium plane irons.
  • ZW-V1KUMIKO candidate. Zen-Wu already dovetail-laminates ZW-V1 into travel-knife edges.
  • Kiri / honokiSaya. Vented. Natural urushi exterior. No Kydex, G10, FRN, TPU, foam, or resin-stabilized wood.
  • KurogakiOptional mechanical inlays. No cord wrap in the food path.
  • NitinolSpinal wires. Nested, drained. Shape-memory category precedent exists at Belmont Japan; this cassette is not their product.
  • DiamondNickel-electroplated monocrystalline on the tag. Zen-Wu already sells swappable-grit cards.
  • Washi, silk, woolPackaging only. They do not enter the river.

Zero plastic in the carried system is a design rule, not a slogan. If a part cannot be titanium, steel, wood, urushi, diamond, silk, washi, wool, or Dyneema, it does not ship.

Steel choice is job-specific. MagnaMax is proposed for the river blade because published testing places its edge-retention / toughness balance near K390 and Vanadis 8 while remaining stainless, with no rust spots in the developer’s 1% saltwater test. That is his laboratory, on his coupons, not a SHINOBI prototype. MagnaCut remains the tougher, already-in-house laminate for the trail blade. Historical sword steel is refused: blood, salt, and fish acid are not a hamon demonstration.

07

Ikejime is a spike. Not the tip.

Driving the knife point through a skull is how you spend the apex on the one job that does not need a cutting edge. The RAILSTONE tag carries a capped, locking brain spike. The tag slides crosswise through the saya to make a T-handle. The knife stays in the tray.

Target anatomy varies by species. The zone on any diagram here is illustrative. Confirm placement with a species guide such as the Ikijime Fish Finder before you use a spike in the field. For large salmon or thick-skulled catfish, use a full-size purpose-built spike and a suitable stunning method. Humane killing is a welfare obligation, not a brand story. Follow current RSPCA-aligned practice: stun, then spike, then wire the canal if you are doing shinkeijime.

1

Stun first. Do not begin with the knife.

2

Uncap the spike. Lock the tag into the saya as a T-handle.

3

Spike the brain at the species-correct site. Recap immediately.

4

Seat S-50 or L-80 in the collet. Run the canal. Rinse the cassette open.

SHINKEI//DUO nests two wires: S-50 (0.8 × 500 mm) for backcountry trout and most bass; L-80 (1.2 × 800 mm) for adult Niagara salmon. Optional XL 1.5 × 800 mm for unusually large Chinook. Tips are rounded and polished so they follow the canal; they are not cutting points. A salmon-capable kit will not remain under one ounce without lying about stiffness or length. NYSDEC describes Great Lakes Chinook commonly around 8–30 pounds, with larger fish possible — the L-80 exists because the S-50 does not.

08

What this system will not do

  • No batoning.
  • No prying, no screwdriver work with the point.
  • No pelvis splitting, no chopping antler or heavy bone.
  • No using the knife as an ikejime spike.
  • No long, uninterrupted one-pass salmon fillets — that is a different blade length.
  • No “best ultralight knife ever” claim. Superlatives are not evidence.
  • No announced price, no inventory, no discount clock, no fake live stock.

Prototype gates before anyone should believe a gram

  1. CAD mass vs. this allocation model, with density of the actual billets.
  2. Four engineering samples: two right, two left, across MIZU and KUMIKO at minimum.
  3. Hardness coupons retained with every heat. MagnaMax window 62.5–63.0 HRC is a target, not a furnace setting.
  4. Reference-face coplanarity, warp, and grind-angle inspection.
  5. Saltwater, blood, and sap sanitation of every cavity and the lamination seam.
  6. Wet-glove retention with and without kurogaki inlays.
  7. Quote ladder at 25 / 50 / 100 before a binding pilot. A 50-unit split (provisionally 40 right / 10 left) is a recommendation, not a purchase order.
09

The tray, not the clamshell

Unboxing is inventory, not theatre. Outer washi sleeve, inner kiri tray, silk wrap, then the modules in labeled recesses: chassis, the blade you ordered, saya, RAILSTONE, film, tweezer, SHINKEI cassette.

Labels, if produced, will be letterpress or branded wood — not a sticker that becomes landfill in the first hour. This is still a study. No SKU, no barcode, no “limited drop.”
