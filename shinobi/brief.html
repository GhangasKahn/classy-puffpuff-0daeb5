01

Design intent

One structure. No universal-edge fiction.

Reduce carried duplication by sharing the handle, retention architecture, edge-care substrate, and protective carry system. Preserve cutting integrity by separating the three blade geometries that the intended work genuinely requires.

  • MIZU–82Controlled skin release, fish and food work, light field utility, and EDC.
  • KUMIKO–42Dead-flat registration for layout, paring, chamfering, and kumiko fitting.
  • NATA–60Supported single-bevel geometry for whittling, notching, and controlled trail cuts.
02

System targets

Targets to challenge—not specifications to trust.

Every figure below is provisional. CAD mass properties, maker review, measured samples, and destructive tests take precedence.

KUMIKO geometry42 mm target blade; 15° target single flat bevel; dead-flat back; fully mirrored hand versions.
NATA geometry60 mm target blade; 24° target single bevel; supported heel; flat reverse reference.

Material ambitionNo polymer in the proposed production object or packaging, contingent on sanitation, retention, durability, and shipping tests.

04

Non-negotiable limits

Small and exact is not indestructible.

  • No heavy impactNo batoning, pelvis splitting, antler work, heavy bone chopping, or uncontrolled free-swing chopping.
  • No leveringNo prying, joint twisting, screwdriver use, or lateral abuse of the point.
  • No false flexibility claimAn 82 mm semi-flex profile does not replace a dedicated long salmon fillet knife.
  • No knife-point ikejimeThe brain spike remains a separate capped tool study. Species location and humane technique require independent instruction.
  • No universal shinkeijime wireWire length and diameter must follow fish size, anatomy, and practiced technique.
05

Four-sample study

Test the architecture before discussing inventory.

  1. Sample setTwo dominant-hand and two fully mirrored engineering samples; exact module mix set after maker review.
  2. Dimensional inspectionMass, back flatness, bevel angle, behind-edge thickness, warp, lock engagement, retention, and hand clearance.
  3. Material inspectionBatch coupon, recorded heat treatment, individual hardness, edge-rail inspection, finish thickness, and corrosion baseline.
  4. Use testingWet-glove retention, cleanup, salt exposure, fish and food work, edge stability, wood registration, carving, drop, and flex.
  5. Failure recordPhotograph and retain failures. Revise geometry before discussing a 25, 50, or 100-unit pilot.
06

Questions for the maker

What should change before a quote?

  1. Which part of the proposed module-lock and tang architecture is least credible at this mass?
  2. Which edge-steel and titanium-lamination combinations fit current shop capability without an unjustified process leap?
  3. What minimum wall, web, shoulder, and internal-radius dimensions would you require before analysis?
  4. Can the KUMIKO module retain a truly flat back after heat treatment, lamination, finishing, and repeated sharpening?
  5. What sanitation or corrosion path has this study overlooked?
  6. Would four samples answer the important questions, and what test coupons or fixtures should accompany them?
