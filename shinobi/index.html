二 · Recognition

You already know the compromise.

A bird-and-trout knife that cannot register a square. A marking knife that cannot live in a river bag. A fillet knife that weighs more than the trout. The usual answer is to carry all three — or to buy a single blade that lies about what it is.

Cold Steel’s Bird & Game is the honest mass benchmark: a 3.5-inch blade listed at 1.4 oz, with a simple full tang and a polymer grip. That efficiency is real. The materials, the grind, and the shop life are not.

Zen-Wu already makes the other pole: a Shirabiki-style marking knife with a titanium-alloy handle and a dovetail-laminated MagnaCut edge, described as about the weight of a pencil, with left- and right-hand versions. It is a layout instrument. It is not a fish knife, and it is not this kit.

The hidden cost is not only grams. It is the moment on a Niagara salmon when the marking knife is at home, or the moment at the kumiko jig when the hunting knife steers off the line.

River bag

Salt, slime, and blood punish hidden cavities, cord wrap, leather, and magnets. A thick hunter steers off bone. A long fillet knife is the right tool for a one-pass salmon side — and the wrong tool to carry for trout, panfish, and elk seam work at once.

Bench

Joinery wants a dead-flat back and a bevel you can put down the same way every time. A hollow that helps fish can rock on a square. A convex “does everything” grind makes repeatable kumiko fitting a matter of hope.

三 · Revelation

Stop asking one grind to tell the truth twice.

The handle is a kusabi — a titanium wedge lock. Blades seat the way a Japanese plane iron seats: mechanically, inspectably, without adhesive. Each iron is allowed to be itself. The kit’s luxury is not ornament. It is the refusal of plastic, of trapped cavities, and of a geometry that exists only in copy.

    Two grips, already carried

    A permanently razor-thin skeleton is honest for pinbones and a pencil grip. It is not honest for a twenty-minute elk seam. The saya is the second handle: it snaps over the frame, fills the apertures, and becomes a drained oval palm swell. You do not carry a spare set of scales. You carry the sheath you already needed.

    Precision mode is bare metal, 4.2–4.8 mm, with a forward pinch saddle and no painful jimping. Power mode is the saya. Both must rinse clean. No cord. No resin wood. No G10.

    Ikejime, not the knife point

    The knife tip is for tissue, joinery, and food. It is a poor brain spike. A dedicated, capped, locking spike rides in the sheath. After brain destruction, a nested spinal wire uses the diamond tag as a T-handle. This is for humane dispatch of fish for food, by adults, where lawful.

    1. Stun where required

      Large salmon and thick-skulled catfish may need a full-size stunning tool. This kit’s spike is not a substitute for proper equipment on those fish.

    2. Brain spike, capped until use

      Confirm the target with a species reference such as the Ikijime Fish Finder. The marked zone on any drawing is illustrative, not universal.

    3. Spinal wire

      S-50 (0.8 × 500 mm) for backcountry trout and most bass. L-80 (1.2 × 800 mm) for adult Niagara salmon. Optional XL 1.5 × 800 mm is named for unusually large Chinook and is not in the lightest loadout. Shape-memory field wires in these diameters already exist commercially (e.g. Belmont Japan); this module is a nested cassette, not a claim to that brand.

    4. Bleed and ice

      Follow a humane, lawful sequence. RSPCA-aligned guidance treats brain destruction plus bleeding as the ethical core — not a cinematic gesture.

    Refusals

    The blade will not

    Baton, pry, split a pelvis, chop antler or heavy bone, open tins as a screwdriver, or make a long one-pass salmon fillet. A saw or axe does heavy wood. A dedicated fillet knife does uninterrupted sides.

    The copy will not

    Call this the lightest knife ever made, a guaranteed sushi edge, or a Zen-Wu product. Named loadout masses (17.52 g pocket through 58.36 g studio) are geometric-model targets from the design brief, not shipping weights.

    The character will not

    Wear kanji as costume, grow a tsuba, or quote an action film. Quiet, exact, washable. If it looks tactical, it is the wrong object.

    五 · Resolution

    If this is a tool Luke would sign, it should be made. If it is not, it should stop here.

    The next step is not a checkout. It is a production conversation: four engineering samples (two right, two left), then quotes at 25, 50, and 100 before anyone pretends a batch exists. A 50-unit pilot — provisionally 40 right and 10 left — is a recommendation, not a purchase order.

    Gate 1 · Samples

    Two right, two left. CAD before metal. Coupon hardness with the batch, not furnace folklore.

    Gate 2 · Abuse

    Wood scribe, wet glove, salt, bone contact, lock cycle, rinse. Fail the lock in the shop, not on a fish.

    Gate 3 · Quote

    25 / 50 / 100. No binding commitment until the samples are the object, not the drawing.

    Gate 4 · Pilot

    Recommended 50 if the quotes and the samples agree. Handedness split is a guess until orders exist.
