1Executive summary

This report delivers a buildable, winter-removable Wright light-screen for a 143″ × 65″ site opening that crosses the driveway: FreeCAD model, dimensioned blueprints, cut list with board feet, joinery vocabulary, live planters as ballast, and a Tree of Life gate flush to the house.

The design problem. The existing run is 143″ long and 65″ high. It must be entirely freestanding — no post-hole digging, no cement, no massive stone or gravel pads — and solid in Buffalo wind, frost, and wet–dry cycling; include a locking privacy gate right next to the house; sit on the driveway slab; contain a dog 100%; and come apart so the timber can be stored for winter. Aesthetic: Prairie School / Darwin Martin House (visited) — if you don’t build it, it never exists — golden-ratio proportions where they do not thin structure, Japanese hand joinery, wood + lighting + a gate latch, painted owner gray. Not a Home Depot ranch fence.

The answer — MARTIN Rev F (primary for this site):

  • Four 4×6 piers tenoned into a timber dodai ladder that sits on the existing driveway. P1–P3 wrapped in Roman-brick 2×2.
  • Cantilevered 2×12 eave + hanging fascia; two 2×10 belts that project 3″ past the piers; three recessed muntin cassettes (nested-rects / Tree of Life / nested-rects).
  • Solid 2×12 PT water table as the dog crawl stop; 1.50″ max pattern aperture.
  • 36″ Tree of Life gate flush to the house (no planter at P0), oak pivots at P1, sliding oak latch into P0. No Z-brace on the garden face.
  • Two live planters (privacy bays only) as wind ballast; optional stone inside the boxes for drainage and mass.
  • Cap soffit 12V IP65 lighting. Knock-down winter sequence. No holes, no cement, no pad.

Related systems archived in this suite: STELE (permanent masonry-pier fence for longer runs) and HASHIRA (standalone joinery reinforcement method). Use them if you later extend beyond 143″ or reinforce another fence.

Status — Rev F. Darwin Martin Tree of Life light-screen on the Rev D sit-on-grade ladder. Rev E stacked 2×10/2×6 bands are retired as a ranch-fence face. Parametric kernel remains the single source of truth. Not a stamped PE document — confirm Green Code and engineer review if the city requires it.

Gate lock options

  • Latch B (default): 1.5″ × 3.5″ × 18″ oak sliding bar into a mortise in latch post P0 with gravity catch — fully freestanding. Cross-peg + optional keyed padlock hasp. No epoxy into the house.
  • Latch A (opt-in only): Same bar into an oak strike you add later on a wall you own. Not in the default kit.
  • Hinges: 1.25″ oak pivots (W-003) — bottom in the sill at P1, top in the cap soffit. Leaf weight in compression to the driveway. Optional stainless pintle is backup only. P0 has no planter, so the gate is not hung as a cantilever off the house post.

6Engineering rationale

Why MARTIN stands in wind without nails

Wind on a 65″ × 143″ face delivers shear and overturning into the posts. Through-bands (nuki) turn that shear into wood-on-wood compression inside the posts; wedges keep the bands from walking. Remaining bands are housed 0.75″ so the ¾″ light-screen gaps do not leave a ¾″ web. Overturning is resisted by the 36″ ladder spread plus wet soil and in-box stone in two garden-side planters (planning 1522 lb vs 1311 lb required at FS 1.5). There is no nail withdrawal path to fatigue in freeze-thaw.

Why the ladder matters

A fence that is not level racks its joints on day one. Two 4×6 dodai sills and four cross-ties create a single plane that sits on the driveway slab (photos). Rubber furniture pads protect the pavement. Packing cribs exist only if outriggers leave the slab. Nothing is poured.

Why winter removal is structural, not a compromise

Buffalo snowplows and ice berms destroy fences that stay at the curb. Designing the timber as a wedge-locked knock-down furniture fence means it survives by leaving. Empty the planters, lift the ladder, store dry. Nothing remains in the ground.

STELE archive notes (Egypt / Rome / Maya)

For permanent longer runs, STELE's battered piers keep bed joints in compression, 48″ drained footings beat frost, and raised curbs keep wood out of snowpack — see the STELE design page. STELE is a different project; MARTIN does not borrow its foundations.

Failure-mode audit (MARTIN)

Wind racking → nuki compression + ladder spread + live planter mass. Dog crawl → PT kick + ¾″ gaps + 0.375″ gate bottom. Rot → pads + PT splash + paint + air gap at planters. Fastener corrosion → no fasteners in timber. Plow impact → fence stored indoors. Theft / privacy → locking latch into P0. Hinge racking → oak pivots into the sill, not a pintle off P0.

10Permits & code path — Buffalo

  • Confirm parcel zoning in the Buffalo Green Code — residential fences commonly limited to 6′ side/rear; 65″ panel height is designed to comply. Front-yard limits can be lower — verify before building.
  • Removable freestanding furniture fences are often treated more lightly than permanent masonry, but ask Permit & Inspection Services if a permit is required.
  • This build does not excavate. 811 (Dig Safely New York) is not required for a sit-on-grade ladder.
  • Latch B is the default: oak bar into P0. Do not drill the house unless you later opt into Latch A on a wall you own.
  • Historic district rules if applicable (Darwin Martin neighborhood context — check).
  • This package is not a stamped engineering document. Hire a NY PE if the city requests one.
