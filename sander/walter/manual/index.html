00 Before you cut

Read the engineering report once. Buy plywood before hardware — voids in a 5×5 sheet become vibration in a 5″ drum. Verify every catalog number on W-14 before you click buy.

  • Three ¾″ Baltic birch 5×5 (or 5×10) + one ¼″. Not MDF. Not construction plywood.
  • Hard maple for base rails and Acme nut blocks.
  • ¾″ × 22″ 1144 Stressproof, keyed 3/16″ × 4″ both ends.
  • Dust collector that actually pulls 400 CFM at 4″. A shop-vac is not enough.
01

Cut and bore 21 discs

Print template T-DISC at 100% (W-10). Check the 1.00″ scale bar. Bandsaw Ø 5.125″ blanks from ¾″ Baltic birch. Bore 0.748″ on the drill press with a fence so every hole shares a center. File a 3/16″ keyway. Number the discs 1–21.

Sheet: W-3 · Template: T-DISC

02

Glue the stack on the keyed shaft

Dry-stack on the ¾″ shaft with the square key. 0.5 mm gaps every four discs (business-card feeler). End bells ⅛″ aluminum. Clamp as a column, not in a vise that bananas the shaft. After cure, turn the OD to 5.000″ ±0.010 between centers — or later, in the machine, on a carrier board.

03

Glue doubled walls

Grain crossed, on a flat door. Idle and drive walls are ¾″ + ¾″. Use the ¼″ steel plates as drill jigs. Idle plate: slot the two bolt holes 0.90″ vertically. Drive plate: round holes, fixed. Datum D2 lives here.

Sheet: W-2 · Template: T-PLATE

04

Square the box

Stretchers, maple base rails, base panel. Datum D1 is the base top. Winding sticks on the bearing-plate faces. If a wall is in wind, the drum will sand a stripe on every board. This is the machine’s accuracy — glue does not forgive it later.

05

Torsion-box platen + ways

¼″ skins over a ¾″ grid. UHMW in dados. Maple nut blocks, dual ¾-6 Acme, HTD 5 mm sync (or #25 chain). Clock the nuts (D4) before the belt. Digital caliper on the idle wall. Lock knobs. Confirm 4.50″ of parallel travel.

Sheet: W-4

06

Turn crowns and track the belt

2.00″ aluminum tube × 16.25″, 0.030″ barrel crown on both rollers. Endless 16×60 2-ply PVC — not a sanding belt. Skew screws on the idle end. Track empty 60 s, then with a 16″ board. PWM feed last. Or skip this step and run Option B: HDPE sliding table.

Sheet: W-5

07

Hinge plate, motor, belt, guard

1 HP TEFC 56C on a ¼″ hinge plate. Turnbuckle tension — not motor weight on a dowel. 3.00″ / 4.75″ 4L pulleys, 4L440 link belt. Full ¼″ birch guard; no finger slot at the pinch. Magnetic starter + OL + mushroom E-stop. Optional hood interlock.

Sheets: W-6 · W-12

08

Hood, 4″ port, brush strip

¼″ birch inverted-U, foam weatherstrip to the walls, nylon brush at the infeed lip. 4″ port aimed at a collector that can pull 400 CFM. Collector on before any test cut.

Sheet: W-7

09

Wrap paper and true the drum

4″ PSA hook tape, joints staggered. 3″ roll, ~17° spiral, start in the idle-end bell slot. Trace the first wrap for replacements. True on a carrier board until the whole face cuts. Number the grit on the idle bell.

Sheet: W-13

10

Jack parallelism

Idle jack at zero (D3). 16″ test board should kiss both ends. Then ¼-20 jacks ±0.040″ if one end is proud. Do not chase this with shims under a flange bearing — that is what Walters had to do.

11

Inspection Q01–Q10

Work inspection.csv. E-stop blink test: kill power, restore power — the drum must not restart. Guard closed. Hood pulling. Acme witness marks aligned.

12

Post the startup card

Collector → hood → lock → drum → feed. Never start with a board under the drum. First passes 0.010″ in poplar, then oak. If the motor note changes, you took too much. This is not a planer. Nothing shorter than 12″ unless it is stuck to a carrier.
