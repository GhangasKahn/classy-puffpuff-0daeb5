01 Lineage

ShopNotes issue 86 (2006) published a drum sander that sat on a table saw. Ron Walters built it, then rebuilt it, and published both the woodgears write-up and the YouTube walkthrough W-5Sj6kBVic. Those two documents are the same machine. This report is original engineering from the failure modes they made public.

ShopNotes #86 remains copyrighted. Do not scan it, do not redraw it, do not treat this package as a reprint. Heslop (crowned plywood rollers, short-stock kickback) and Hunt (open C-frame) informed options, not geometry.

What Walters kept, we kept: a dedicated motor, flange bearings, a wrap drum, and a dust hood that actually works. What failed three times — conveyor tracking — is the whole reason the rollers are aluminum with a 0.030″ barrel crown.

1427 SFM is the same neighborhood as a 16–32 SuperMax. Fast enough for 80-grit thicknessing, not so fast that 150-grit burns. Walters’ ⅓ HP survived whisper-thin hand-fed passes. A powered 16″ feed at 10 FPM in oak will stall that motor. Spec 1 HP; treat 1.5 HP as luxury.

Rotation

Bottom of the drum moves toward infeed (against feed). Dust goes into the back hood. Reverse this and you throw grit at the operator and glaze the paper.

Drum stack

Twenty-one ¾″ Baltic birch discs, blanked Ø 5.125″, bored 0.748″, keyed 3/16″. 0.5 mm glue-line gap every fourth disc so seasonal movement does not split the stack the way MDF did. End bells are ⅛″ aluminum with retainer slots. True the OD to 5.000″ ±0.010 after glue. Square key, not piano wire — removable, kinder to 1144 Stressproof.

Conveyor

A sanding belt is designed to track on a crowned drive pulley with a slack idler. Walters used a 16×48 sanding belt as a conveyor on oval PVC. It never tracked. WALTER uses a 16×60 2-ply PVC conveyor belt on 2.00″ aluminum rollers with a 0.030″ barrel crown and idle-end skew screws. Track empty for 60 seconds, then with a 16″ board. Option B is an HDPE sliding table — skip the belt entirely if tracking still fights you.

03 Datums D1–D4

The machine is only as true as four references. Square the box before glue. Drill both bearing plates from one story stick.

  1. D1 — Base top face = Z0. Walls sit on it. Stretchers must not lift a wall. Check with winding sticks.
  2. D2 — Drum axis = Z 13.50″, Y 18.00″. Idle and drive plates share one story stick. Idle plate holes are slotted 0.90″ vertically for the jack.
  3. D3 — Idle jack at zero = plates coplanar. True the drum, then jack for parallelism against a 16″ test board. Do not chase taper with shims.
  4. D4 — Acme nuts clocked together before the HTD belt goes on. Paint a witness mark. Four independent screws will rack the platen into a wedge.

Inspection Q01–Q10

The fab package writes fab/12_QA/inspection.csv. Hold Q02 (drum OD), Q03 (parallelism), Q04 (belt tracking), and Q06 (E-stop, no auto-restart) before any oak.

05 CAD pipeline

One parameter dict. Meshes, OpenSCAD, FreeCAD, plan sheets, and fab CSVs all read it.

sander/walter/cad/walter_kernel.py emits boxes and cylinders in inches. scripts/gen_walter_cad.py tessellates binary STL (no FreeCAD required), Wavefront OBJ, isometric SVG renders, and parameters.json / parameters.scad. OpenSCAD includes the generated SCAD. FreeCAD walter_sander.py imports the same primitives and writes STEP/FCStd when freecadcmd exists.
