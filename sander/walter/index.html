02 — Lineage, not a reprint

ShopNotes #86 failed in public. We redesigned the failures.

The 2006 magazine machine sat on a table saw. Ron Walters published what happened when he made it a real tool. The YouTube walkthrough is the same machine. This package is original engineering from those notes — not a copy of copyrighted drawings.

Walters · woodgears

What he got right

Dedicated ⅓ HP motor instead of stealing the table saw. Flange bearings in single-skin Baltic birch. Velcro-wrap drum. Dust hood that actually works. He sanded rough oak to planer-grade in light passes at ~1100 RPM.

Video · W-5Sj6kBVic

What the camera showed

Motor hanging on a wooden dowel goes slack on startup. Idle bearing needed shim stock. Formica platen scored. MDF discs hairline-cracked. The conveyor — a 16×48 sanding belt on PVC — was rebuilt three times and never tracked.

WALTER · this machine

What we changed

1 HP, hinged tension, crowned aluminum rollers, PVC conveyor belt, dual Acme + digital readout, idle-end jack screws, birch drum with expansion gaps and paper-retainer slots, HDPE platen, magnetic starter.

03 — Numbers that have to be true

Surface speed, feed, and a drum that stays round

Drum speed1089RPM = 1725 × 3.00 / 4.75
Surface speed1427SFM = π × 5.00 × 1089 / 12
Feed at 30 rpm15.7FPM = π × 2.00 / 12 × 30
Elevation0.167inches per Acme revolution

1427 SFM is the same neighborhood as a 16–32 SuperMax. It is fast enough for 80-grit thicknessing and not so fast that 150-grit burns. ⅓ HP survived because Walters took whisper passes by hand. A powered 16″ feed at 10 FPM in oak will stall that motor. Spec 1 HP; treat 1.5 HP as luxury.

Drum inertia
Birch stack ~8 lb at 5″ Ø. Keep the key. Do not run an unbalanced blank.
Expansion
0.5 mm glue-line gap every fourth disc. MDF cracked without this.
Bearing load
Self-aligning ¾″ inserts in ¼″ plates. Idle slots take jack-screw motion.
Platen flatness
A 0.010″ hump under the drum prints as a stripe on every board.
Crown
0.030″ barrel on both 2″ rollers. Flat rollers do not self-center. PVC is not round.
Rotation
Bottom of drum moves toward infeed (against feed), dust into the back hood.

07 — Build sequence

True the drum last. Track the belt empty first.

Phase 1

Drum

Bandsaw 21 discs Ø 5.125″. Bore 0.748″ on the drill press. Dry-stack on the keyed shaft with 0.5 mm gaps every four discs. Glue. True between centers or in the machine after the frame exists.

Phase 2

Frame

Glue doubled walls, grain crossed, on a flat door. Steel plates as drill jigs. Idle wall gets vertical slots and jack screws; drive wall is fixed. Square the box before the glue grabs — this is the machine's accuracy.

Phase 3

Table

Torsion-box platen, UHMW ways, nut blocks, Acme screws, HTD sync, digital caliper on the idle wall. Lock knobs. Confirm 4.5″ of parallel travel.

Phase 4

Conveyor

Turn 0.030″ crowns. Mount rollers. Skew hardware on the idle end. Endless PVC belt. Track empty, then loaded. PWM feed last. Or skip this phase and run Option B: HDPE sliding table.

Phase 5

Drive & dust

Hinge plate, motor, pulleys, link belt, turnbuckle, full guard. Wire magnetic starter and E-stop. Hood, 4″ port, brush strip. Dust collector on before any test cut.

Phase 6

Tune

Wrap paper. True the drum on a carrier board. Jack the idle bearing until a 16″ test board kisses both ends. 0.010″ passes in poplar, then oak. If the motor note changes, you took too much.

  • Never sand pieces shorter than 12″ unless they are stuck to a carrier.
  • Never start the drum with a board already under it.
  • This is not a planer. Light passes until it is boring.
